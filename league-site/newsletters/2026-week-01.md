Week 1: Somebody had to win the Dumpster Fire
Pisskissing Supreme sets the pace, the two most recent champions play the ugliest game of the week, and Kraken's bench outscores much of the league.

Hope everyone had a great Week 1. One week down with many more to go. Let's take a look at what happened.

🏆 Team of the Week: Pisskissing Supreme
After Week 1, there's a clear leader in the clubhouse.

Huge performances from Jalen Hurts, Kenneth Walker, Isaiah Likely, David Montgomery, and Jonathan Taylor carried Pisskissing Supreme to a league-high 168.4 points, and that came despite his WR1 and WR2 combining for just 6.8 points.

Team Johnson put up a respectable 111.0, but there was never much of a chance of keeping pace.

It's an emphatic start to the season, especially against the only team in the league to make the playoffs three straight years.

If the receivers ever decide to show up, this team could get scary fast.

🗑️ Dumpster Fire of the Week: Deez Fadez 98.4 — Mele 96.1
The Week 1 matchup between defending champion Mele and 2024 champ Deez Fadez had all the makings of a big time matchup.

Instead, we got whatever this was.

Deez Fadez somehow escaped with a win despite Matthew Stafford putting up an abysmal 4 points at quarterback. Normally, that should be enough to bury your week. Apparently not when your opponent is equally committed to losing.

Mele managed just 96.1 points, and that was with Trevor Lawrence dropping 26.

The closest matchup of Week 1 was also somehow the worst. Neither team deserved the win, but one of them had to get it, and at the end of the day, that's all that matters.

🐙 Kraken Unleashed: Kraken 139.4 — Team Manning 127.0
One of the better matchups of the week.

Team Manning put up a solid 127 even though Colston Loveland was about as effective as a screen door on a submarine, but the Kraken was officially unleashed, dropping 139.4 and taking care of business.

Both of these teams would have beaten most of the league this week, but the craziest part is that Kraken somehow left even more points sitting on the bench.

Caleb Williams dropped 37 and Jalen Coker put up 33, and neither one even made the starting lineup.

Despite scoring 139.4, Kraken also had by far the highest scoring bench in the league at an absurd 111 points. Garth's bench alone would have beaten a handful of teams this week.

Safe to say, Kraken might have some lineup decisions to make going into Week 2.

💥 Blowout of the Week: Team Forde 152.6 — Mike's Monstrous Team 86.6
There are fantasy losses.

And then there's getting absolutely bent over by 66 points.

Team Forde dropped 152.6, the second highest score of the week, while Mike's Monstrous Team somehow managed just 86.6.

Calling this a matchup feels generous. By the middle of Sunday afternoon, Mike wasn't playing fantasy football anymore. He was just taking it up the ass for four quarters and hoping nobody checked the score.

At some point, this stopped being competitive and turned into a wellness check.

Final margin: 66.0 points.

Mike, I hope you made some moves on the waiver wire, because after Week 1, this roster needs more than a tune up.

🌱 Employee of the Week: Team Payne 146.0 — 5280 124.4
Team Payne had one of the strongest performances of Week 1, dropping 146 points and putting up a score that would have beaten 10 of the other 11 teams in the league.

5280, run by Carson, actually had a solid week with 124.4 points.

Unfortunately for Carson, he just happened to run into his boss.

Losing in fantasy is one thing. Losing to the guy you have to see at work the next morning makes it sting a little more.

Nothing says "great start to the week" like taking a loss on Sunday, then showing up Monday and getting right back to working with the guy who handed you the loss.

Payne cuts the grass. Carson got cut down.

👻 Joe's Not So Scary Team: Team Szub 121.4 — Joe's Scary Team 106.4
Despite the name, Joe's Scary Team was anything but scary in Week 1. The only frightening part was watching them limp to a disappointing 106.4 points.

Joe may need to revisit the name.

Team Szub, meanwhile, quietly handled business with 121.4 points, thanks largely to Josh Allen dropping 35. That performance helped cover up some brutal outings from Ja'Marr Chase and Rico Dowdle.

It wasn't flashy, but a win is a win. Team Szub starts the year 1-0 while Joe's Scary Team has some work to do before anyone starts getting scared.

Good luck in Week 2, everyone.
